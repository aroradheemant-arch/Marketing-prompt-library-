# Prompt Library — Meridian Digital (Digital Marketing Agency)

*A 20-person agency serving SME clients (retail, hospitality, professional services) across social media, content, SEO, paid media and reporting.*

| # | Workflow | Prompt | Problem Solved | Automation Potential | Risks & Limitations |
|---|----------|--------|-----------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| 1 | Social captions (multi-client) | "Social copywriter for [client]. Write 3 IG captions (≤150 chars) for [topic], tone [X], + 2 hashtags + CTA." | Manual captions take ~20–30 min/client/day. | High — ~70% faster first drafts. | May miss brand nuance; human review before posting. |
| 2 | Paid ad A/B testing | "Write 5 headline (≤30 chars) + description (≤90 chars) variants for [platform] ad promoting [offer] to [audience], testing [angle]." | Manual variant-writing limits test volume, slows launches. | High — more variants tested, no extra headcount. | Risk of generic copy; claims need legal/platform-policy check. |
| 3 | Monthly client reporting | "Given this data: [metrics], write a 150-word client summary: what happened, why, one next step. No jargon." | Turning metrics into client-ready narrative is slow, inconsistent. | High — standardises tone, frees analyst time. | Can misread trends/causation; figures need human verification. |
| 4 | SEO blog briefing | "Create a blog brief for '[keyword]': title, 3 H2s, word count, 50-word meta description, for [audience]." | Briefing writers consistently is time-consuming for a small team. | Medium-high — speeds briefing; writers still draft content. | SEO data can date fast; check against current search intent. |
| 5 | Email nurture sequences | "Draft a 3-email sequence (subject + 100 words each) moving [audience] from [trigger] to [goal], for [client]." | Sequences are repetitive to write per client/campaign. | High — fast drafts; needs a brand-voice pass. | Generic tone risk; must meet consent/anti-spam rules. |
| 6 | Meeting follow-up | "From these notes: [paste], list action items (owner, due date), then draft a 100-word client follow-up email." | Follow-ups are often delayed or inconsistent after calls. | High — near-instant, consistent follow-up. | May miss meeting nuance/tone; needs a human read-through. |
| 7 | Competitor research | "Summarise these competitors' recent posts [links/paste]: themes, posting frequency, one gap [client] could exploit." | Manual competitor tracking is time-heavy and inconsistent. | Medium — solid first pass; strategist adds judgement. | Can't verify live data; may cite stale campaigns. |
| 8 | Audience persona synthesis | "From this research [paste notes], synthesise one persona: goals, pain points, channels, one messaging implication." | Turning raw research into usable personas is slow. | Medium — speeds synthesis; strategist validates against reality. | Risk of over-generalising from a small sample. |
| 9 | Creative brief generation | "From this kickoff summary [paste], draft a 1-page brief: objective, audience, key message, tone, deliverables, deadline." | Inconsistent briefs across account managers cause creative rework. | High — standardises briefs, cuts revision cycles. | Gaps in the call become gaps in the brief; needs AM sign-off. |
| 10 | Reputation/crisis response | "Draft 2 response options (professional, empathetic, ≤80 words) to this review [paste], for [client]. No liability admissions. Check both against [client]'s tone guide and revise any that don't fit." | Slow/inconsistent responses can escalate reputational damage. | Low-medium — drafts only; always needs senior approval. | High reputational/legal risk if unsupervised; never auto-publish. |

**Iteration evidence:** Prompts 1 and 3 were revised after an initial test round — v1 drafts lacked format and tone constraints, producing inconsistent output. Prompt 10 was revised to add a refinement step (self-check against the tone guide before returning a draft).
